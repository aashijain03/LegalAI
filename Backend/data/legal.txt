A legal notice is a formal communication sent before initiating legal proceedings. Receiving a legal notice does not automatically mean a lawsuit has been filed.

A person receiving a legal notice should carefully read it and respond within the time mentioned in the notice whenever appropriate.

Ignoring a legal notice may result in civil or criminal proceedings depending on the nature of the dispute.

Loan disputes are generally civil matters unless fraud, cheating, or criminal breach of trust is involved.

A cheque returned unpaid due to insufficient funds may attract proceedings under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881.

The limitation period for filing a money recovery suit is generally three years from the date the money became due, subject to applicable law.

Under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, consumers can seek compensation for defective goods, deficient services, unfair trade practices, and misleading advertisements.

Consumers should preserve invoices, receipts, emails, messages, and payment proofs while filing a consumer complaint.

Tenants and landlords should execute a written rent agreement to avoid future disputes.

Eviction procedures generally require compliance with applicable rent control laws and due legal process.

Property buyers should verify ownership documents, encumbrance certificates, and approvals before purchasing immovable property.

A First Information Report (FIR) is generally registered for cognizable offences by the police.

For non-cognizable offences, the police may record a Non-Cognizable Report (NCR) and seek directions from the Magistrate where required.

Anyone arrested has the right to know the grounds of arrest.

An arrested person has the right to consult and be defended by a lawyer of their choice.

A person produced after arrest should ordinarily be presented before the nearest Magistrate within 24 hours, excluding travel time, unless otherwise permitted by law.

Bail is a legal process through which an accused person may seek release from custody subject to applicable legal provisions.

Domestic violence victims can seek protection, residence orders, monetary relief, custody orders, and compensation under the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005.

Dowry demand is prohibited under the Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961.

Sexual harassment at the workplace is prohibited under the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013.

Every organization meeting the legal threshold should constitute an Internal Committee to address workplace sexual harassment complaints.

Divorce in India is governed by personal laws applicable to the parties depending on their religion and other applicable statutes.

Parents have a legal obligation to maintain their minor children.

Senior citizens may seek maintenance from their children under applicable laws.

A will allows a person to specify how their property should be distributed after death.

Driving without a valid driving licence is punishable under the Motor Vehicles Act.

Every motor vehicle used in a public place should generally have valid registration and insurance.

Using another person's copyrighted work without permission may amount to copyright infringement unless permitted by law.

Trademark registration helps protect a brand name, logo, or symbol from unauthorized use.
